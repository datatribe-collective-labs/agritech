# **2\. Business idea**

## **Business idea**

Relate is a mobile application that provides relational planting suggestions for gardens and farms, initially focusing on outdoor crops. The app recommends plant groups and combinations based on location, climate, weather, and soil data. Relate uses companion planting information collected by the IDEP foundation and other online sources, and over time will also incorporate stories and experience reports from gardeners and farmers about different plant groups and combinations.

# **3\. Products and services**

## **Products and services**

For the MVP, Relate provides relational planting recommendations based on location and environment data, delivered through a mobile app. Early users primarily have access to the app, and the team will continue discussions with them to take their growing experiences into account when developing Relate. In later versions, people who share their planting and growing experiences through Relate will have more opportunities to be heard, although the exact mechanisms for this have not yet been defined.

## **Pricing principles**

The preferred model is for Relate to be a free, open app for users rather than a traditional SaaS subscription service. The project does not yet have a final financing model. To keep access free, Relate aims to work with funding partners and research groups who can analyse planting data collected through the app and support the development of the service.

At the beginning, we would offer small case-by-case pilot sums or in joint funding applications where the costs of Relate's data collection are included in the project. Later on, we could offer data licenses or pilot fees to research groups/research projects.

# **4\. Customers, markets and business environment**

## **Customers**

Early users will be individual gardeners and farmers (growers) who test Relate’s MVP in different regions. The first trial users are planned in South Korea, Kenya and Finland.

## **Operating area**

Relate operates globally, starting with pilot users in South Korea, Kenya and Finland.

# **5\. Competition and business environment**

## **Competitors**

When not using Relate, growers typically rely on industrial and monocropping research, local knowledge, and online gardening articles and apps. Some growers also ask for planting-scheduler tools, which Relate has deliberately left outside the MVP scope. In contrast, Relate focuses on relational planting and its potential to support healthy topsoil, for example by using plant combinations that provide ground cover and reduce the need for chemical fertilisers.

## **Competitors' strengths and weaknesses**

*(No content)*

## **Your competitive advantage / differentiation**

Scientific research on intercropping, companion planting and other relational planting techniques is still underdeveloped compared with monoculture and industrial farming research. Relate is therefore an experiment in sharing and collecting relational planting experiences to provide more dynamic and responsive planting data. This kind of data is increasingly needed as climate change leads to more volatile weather patterns.

## **Changes in the business environment**

Soil degradation is an increasing challenge for growers. In addition, many young farmers who relocate from cities to the countryside or outer suburban areas may have lost local connection to growing food and pre-industrial local growing practices.

## **Industry competition situation**

Relational, regenerative and agroecological planting practices are increasingly discussed among growers, but they are not yet mainstream in everyday practice.

# **6\. Marketing and sales**

## **Marketing strategy**

Relate focuses on network-based, experimental marketing. In the MVP stage, the app is openly available for anybody to try, and we connect with growers mainly through chat services, social media and existing grower networks. We also aim to collaborate with companion-planting, permaculture and agroecology organisations such as IDEP, as well as research and funding partners, who can both contribute to the content and help reach new users. Over time, the emphasis remains on reaching growers and communities who are curious about relational and regenerative planting practices, rather than on traditional paid advertising.

## **Sales methods**

The MVP will be available online for anybody to try, and we will use chat services and social media to connect with growers in the early stages.

## **Brand, company image, and customer relationship management**

A digital participatory research programme to develop relational planting as a way to build soil.

# **7\. Operations plan**

## **Startup phases and timeline**

We are currently working on the MVP: public open-access data sources have been found, some Python scripts have been written, and the system and architecture have been planned and drawn up. The next steps are to implement and test the MVP with early users, and to keep refining the app based on their feedback and practical experiments. Relate will be formally established as a company after the MVP has been validated.

## **Key partners and suppliers**

In ongoing operations, the core work of Relate remains with the internal team. Around this core, we aim to collaborate with cloud and technology partners for hosting and infrastructure, research groups and universities interested in analysing relational planting data, and agroecology, permaculture and companion-planting organisations such as IDEP and its peers. Funding partners may also play a role in supporting the analysis and use of collected data and in helping to reach new growers and communities.

## **Production process / service delivery**

For the MVP, we are having informal online discussions with growers, and their responses directly contribute to the app’s direction and development.

## **Permits, certificates, and regulatory requirements**

Relate is a digital application and does not include activities that typically require specific sector permits such as health care, food handling, transport or accommodation services. Before formally establishing the company and launching the service, we will still check the general legal obligations that apply to a digital service, including data protection, contracts and any registrations required for the chosen company form.

## **Premises, equipment, and other resources**

For the MVP, Relate is developed mainly through remote work without fixed premises, using standard laptops, cloud services and online collaboration tools. As a side activity, the team aims to have access to a small garden plot where the app can be tested in practice with real planting experiments.

# **9\. Risks and preparedness**

## **Industry and market risks**

The main industry and market risks for Relate are finding enough growers who want to try the MVP and collaborate with us, so that we can eventually build a responsive system where people share their experiences with relational planting. It will also be challenging to find funding and resources to maintain the work over time. We can minimise some of these risks by working with organisations that focus on agroecology, organic and biodynamic farming, permaculture, agroforestry and related areas such as ‘agriwilding’, which looks for ways to improve biodiversity by growing edible plants.

## **Financial risks**

The main financial risk for Relate is finding stable long-term funding and resources to maintain and develop the work over time. Because the aim is to keep the app free and open, there is a risk that we will not secure sufficient, continuous funding to cover development, hosting and research collaboration costs.

## **Personnel or operational risks**

Relate currently depends on a small core team, which is a personnel risk if key people are unavailable or have limited capacity over time. We will look for people with agroecology and social experience, or people who would like to grow in that direction, and for collaborators who are good at funding applications or can at least help draft applications and navigate possible EU funding.

## **Preparedness measures and risk management**

We aim to minimise costs by using open‑access data and keeping the technical setup as lightweight as possible. The only direct costs are people’s wages and, eventually, data storage and compute. To reduce risks, we collaborate closely with growers and look for partners who can provide resources and support the long‑term development of Relate. Funding could come from foundations, grants or offering case-by-case licenses to research groups working on relational planting.
